# Fiqh of Prayer for the Sick (Salatul Mareed)

Source: General Authority of Islamic Affairs and Endowments (GAIAE), UAE. Rulings of Salah of Sick. 1436 AH / 2015 AD.

## General Principles

Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship (Quran 2:185).

Allah, Exalted be He, said: "Allah intends to make things easy on you, and man has been created weak" (An-Nisa 4:28).

Allah said: "Allah does not like to impose a problem on you" (Al-Ma'idah 5:6).

Allah said: "And do not kill yourselves. Indeed Allah has been Very Merciful to you" (An-Nisa 4:29).

The Islamic jurisprudence (Fiqh) has discussed the rulings pertaining to the sick and laid down special provisions for them, particularly about taharah (cleanliness) and salah (prayer). Remembrance of Allah is healing for souls, peace of mind, and relaxation of the heart.

## Taharah (Purification) for the Sick

### Using Water
If a sick person is able to use water for taharah, he should perform wudu for minor hadath (uncleanness due to passing urine, stool, or wind) and should perform ghusl for major hadath (ritual impurity).

### Tayammum (Dry Ablution)
If a sick person is unable to use water, or did not find a person who can help him to use water, or fears that using water will inflict any harm on him, he will perform tayammum for offering salah whether he is in the state of minor hadath or major hadath.

Tayammum can be observed by striking both the palms on clean soil; the first stroke for wiping over the face and the second stroke for wiping both the hands up to the wrists.

### Assisted Wudu
If it is difficult for a sick person to do wudu or tayammum by himself, someone else can make him do wudu or tayammum and it will suffice for him. However, if he finds no one who can make him do wudu or tayammum, he can perform salah in the same state and then reoffer the prayer when able.

### Impurities on the Body
If a sick person is not able to remove filth, he can perform salah in the same state. If a sick person is suffering from enuresis or other impurities, he will be exempted to cleanse it. If such a condition persists for half of the time or more, his wudu will not be nullified, but it is mustahhab (desirable) to do wudu for each salah.

### Wounds and Dressings
One who is wounded in a traffic accident, had burning, or any other injury, then he can do mas'h (wipe wet hands) over his dressing or plaster; and if the mas'h is harmful, he can do tayammum.

Mas'h is permissible for one who has several wounds in the parts of wudu, though one has minor wounds or in the state of impurity. This is established from the Quran and Sunnah.

It is narrated by Sayyidna Jabir (may Allah be pleased with him): The Prophet (may Allah bless him and grant him peace) permitted a wounded person for tayammum though he is musafir (traveler) and healthy. (Mustadrak 'ala al-Sahihain by al-Hakim)

## Salah of a Sick Person Individually

### Timeliness
A sick person should perform salah in its time and in the same condition in which he is. He is not allowed to delay it with an intention of making up missed salah after being cured from his illness. Allah said: "Surely, Salah is an obligation on the believers that is tied up with time" (An-Nisa 4:103).

### Posture Hierarchy
The prominent characteristic of salah of the sick is that a sick person is exempted to perform the fundamental acts which he is unable to do:

- **Able to stand:** Pray standing.
- **Unable to stand:** Pray sitting.
- **Unable to sit:** Pray lying on side facing qiblah.
- **Unable to lie on side:** Pray lying on back.

Evidence: Imam Bukhari reported that Imran bin Husain (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I was suffering from piles, so I asked the Prophet (may Allah bless him and grant him peace) about the prayer. He said: "Pray standing and if you can't, then pray while sitting and if you can't do even that, then pray lying on your side." (Sahih al-Bukhari: 1117)

### Qiblah Exemption
If a sick person is unable to turn face towards the qiblah by himself or with other's help, he will be exempted to face the qiblah. He will be asked to repeat it if he was able to do it till the end of the time, but not after the time.

### Ruku (Bowing) Exemptions
If a sick person is able to stand in salah but unable to do ruku or sujud, he should perform salah standing and make gesture with head for ruku and sit down, and then make gesture for sujud.

One who is unable to do ruku or sujud both, he should make gesture with head for both and should lower the head more for sujud than ruku.

If one is unable to make gesture with head, he can make gesture with eye while making intention in heart; it will suffice for him.

The mandatory action (rukn) is to bend so that the hands reach the knees if physically capable. If a severe chronic injury prevents this, bending only as much as is safely possible is acceptable while maintaining the mental intention of the pillar. The priority is to avoid exacerbating pain.

### Sujud (Prostration) Exemptions
According to the Prophetic tradition, prostration must be performed on seven "bones" or parts of the body: the forehead (including the nose), the two palms, the two knees, and the pads of the two feet.

If a recent surgical procedure prevents the knees from touching the ground, the worshipper is permitted to sit on a chair and incline their torso forward, making the incline for sujud deeper than the incline for ruku.

If wrist pain or fracture prevents placing the palms flat, the worshipper may use the knuckles, forearms, or a padded support. The essential element is placing the face (forehead and nose) on the ground; hand placement is a confirmed sunnah, not a pillar.

If ankle injury prevents the tops of the feet from touching the ground, the worshipper may use socks, a soft mat, or sit in a chair. The seven bones of prostration are the forehead, nose, two palms, two knees, and two feet — but necessity (darura) permits adaptation.

### Chair Prayer Rules
A sick person performing salah in a chair should follow these points:

- He should sit in the chair from the beginning if he is unable to observe qiyam, ruku, and sujud.
- If he is able to observe qiyam only, not ruku and sujud, then he should observe qiyam standing and then sit for ruku and sujud.
- If he starts salah sitting due to any disability and recovers during the salah, he should stand for the rest of his salah.
- If one starts salah standing, then turns unable to stand during the salah, he should perform the rest of his salah sitting.
- A sick person can offer salah on bed if he is unable to get down.

### Forgetting or Sleeping Through Prayer
If a sick or healthy person slept or forgot salah, he should perform it when he wakes up or when he remembers it. The Prophet (may Allah bless him and grant him peace) said: "One who forgot salah or slept (and missed it), then its expiation is that he should observe it when he remembers it." (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim)

### Unconsciousness
As far as one who is unconscious, he will not have to make up any missed salah since he is mindless. So, if an unconscious person returned to his senses and found time even for wudu and one rak'ah of salah, he will have to offer that rak'ah.

## Salah of Sick with Jama'ah as Muqtadi

A sick person will be excused to be present in mosque for salah with jama'ah (congregation) if it is difficult for him.

A sick person performing salah in chair should follow the imam's movements in congregation.

## Specific Condition Rulings

### Neck Pain
If neck pain prevents the standard head position in any posture, the worshipper may adjust head alignment to avoid pain. The Prophet permitted praying while sitting and leaning when standing was difficult; similar latitude applies to cervical conditions.

### Shoulder Injury
If a shoulder injury prevents placing the hands on the knees during ruku, the worshipper should bend as far as safely possible. The pillar (rukn) is the bowing motion itself, not the hand placement. If the shoulder cannot bear weight in sujud, using a raised support or chair is permitted.

### Back and Spine Conditions
If back pain or spine surgery prevents forward flexion during ruku or sujud, the worshipper should bend only as far as safely possible. The intention and effort fulfill the obligation; complete physical execution is only required if physically possible without harm.

### Hip Conditions
If hip pain or immobility prevents standard prayer postures, the worshipper should sit on a chair. If sitting is also difficult, they may lie on their side.

### Knee Conditions
If knee pain prevents kneeling for sujud, the worshipper may sit on a chair and incline forward. If a recent surgical procedure prevents the knees from touching the ground, sitting on a chair with deeper incline for sujud than for ruku is permitted.

### Leg and Lower Limb Conditions
If leg pain or weakness prevents standing, the worshipper should sit. If sitting is also difficult, they may lie on their side. The principle is to perform the prayer in the manner closest to the standard form that the body can sustain without harm.

### Wrist and Hand Conditions
If wrist pain or fracture prevents placing the palms flat during sujud, the worshipper may use the knuckles, forearms, or a padded support. The essential element is placing the face on the ground; hand placement is a confirmed sunnah, not a pillar.

### Elbow Conditions
If elbow flexion contracture prevents reaching the knees during ruku, the worshipper bends to their maximum safe capacity. The intention and effort to bow fulfill the obligation.

### Ankle and Foot Conditions
If ankle injury prevents the tops of the feet from touching the ground during sujud, the worshipper may use socks, a soft mat, or sit in a chair. Necessity (darura) permits adaptation of the prostration posture.
